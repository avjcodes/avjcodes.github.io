Modular counter kit, Lyle's Den

A pizzeria front counter for a first-person horror project in Unreal Engine 5, built as five modules on a 0.25 m grid: two straights, a filler, an end cap and a corner. One 2K trim sheet covers every piece, and the wear is layered in the order the room aged: sun fade, hand wear, water, then dust last.

LOD0 1,372 to 2,260 tris per module · authored LOD1 and LOD2 · UCX collision · second UV channel · texel density 9.8 px/cm against a 10.24 target, every face inside band · seam gaps 0.000000 m at every join

Characters

Low-poly and PSX-era characters with hand-placed topology, palette or pixel textures, and a rig that survives the export gate. Two enemies for an apartment-block pack were built to read apart at a glance: one tall and gaunt, one short and top-heavy.

How a piece gets built

  • Blockout to real-world scale first, on the client's grid, with pivots agreed before detail.
  • Trim sheets or a shared atlas over per-asset textures whenever a location shares materials.
  • LODs authored, not decimated, so UVs and silhouettes hold up at distance.
  • Collision, second UV channel and naming to the engine's convention on every export.
  • Repetitive passes (variants, LOD chains, export checks) run as Blender scripts, so batches stay consistent.
  • Every delivery re-imported into an empty scene and checked before it is sent.
